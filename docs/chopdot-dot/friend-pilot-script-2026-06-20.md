# ChopDot Friend Pilot Script

Status: `ready-to-run`
Date: 2026-06-20
Programme: `B` product usability + Track 1 readiness

## Purpose

Use this script to test whether friends can use ChopDot without a technical narrator.

The pilot is not asking whether people understand Polkadot. It asks whether they understand:

- who joined,
- who owes,
- who claimed paid,
- who confirmed,
- who approved,
- what is blocked,
- what can close,
- what record can be trusted later.

## Required Setup

Use separate devices or separate browser profiles. Do not role-switch on one shared screen for the real pilot.

Required participants:

- Leo: contributor / payout receiver
- Nina: contributor
- Omar: contributor / payer
- Mina: organizer / treasurer
- Riley: emergency organizer
- Taylor: emergency approver
- Jordan: emergency/community receiver
- Alex: community fund admin
- Priya: community fund approver
- Sam: community contributor / payer

Allowed entry:

- `/pots`
- `/pots?chopdot-dot-native=1&person=<name>`
- capture links generated by the app

Not allowed during the pilot:

- explaining the kernel, Product SDK, Statement Store, Bulletin, adapters, rails, or `.dot` internals;
- telling someone which button to press before they describe what they think the screen is asking;
- using developer checks, role switching, escrow lab controls, or raw JSON as evidence of user understanding.

## Universal Pass Questions

Ask every participant these before they click:

1. What is this group trying to finish?
2. What is your next step?
3. Who is the group waiting on?
4. What would be unsafe to assume from this screen?
5. What record would you trust later?

Facilitator guardrail:

- the participant must understand that claim, confirmation, approval, release, and closeout are separate.

Passing answer must use user language such as:

- `I need to mark paid`
- `Mina needs to confirm`
- `Taylor approves later`
- `Jordan confirms after release`
- `This is not escrow`
- `A payment claim is not confirmation`
- `The receipt is private/redacted`

Failing answer includes:

- `the app already has the money`
- `approval means it was paid`
- `token complete means confirmed`
- `close means everything was paid`
- `this is guaranteed payout`
- `the emergency details are public`

## Scenario 1: Group Expense

Job: friends split a dinner and close a trusted private record.

People:

- Leo pays his share.
- Nina pays her share.
- Omar is late or delayed.
- Mina confirms what arrived.

Steps:

1. Leo opens the dinner split from his own device.
2. Leo explains his next step.
3. Leo clicks `Mark paid`.
4. Leo explains why he is done for now and who still has to confirm.
5. Nina opens from her own device and marks paid.
6. Mina opens from her own device.
7. Mina explains the organizer queue.
8. Mina confirms Leo and Nina only if she says money arrived.
9. Mina records Omar as delayed.
10. Mina closes with an open-item note only if the UI says that is allowed.
11. Mina opens the receipt.

Pass if:

- Leo and Nina find `Mark paid` without coaching.
- Leo can say `Mina still needs to confirm`.
- Mina can explain `Confirm only if money arrived`.
- Omar's delay is visible before closeout.
- The receipt makes clear what was confirmed and what was noted.

Fail if:

- anyone thinks `Mark paid` means Mina confirmed receipt;
- Mina closes without noticing Omar is delayed;
- the receipt hides the open item or suggests everything was paid.

## Scenario 2: Savings Circle

Job: run a recurring contribution round with payout order and delay handling.

People:

- Leo contributes and receives this round's payout.
- Nina contributes.
- Omar is delayed or records release later.
- Mina is treasurer.

Steps:

1. Leo opens `Friday savings circle`.
2. Leo says the contribution amount and who confirms.
3. Leo clicks `Mark paid`.
4. Leo explains why he cannot confirm his own contribution.
5. Nina opens from her own device and marks paid.
6. Mina opens and reads the organizer queue.
7. Mina confirms Leo and Nina.
8. Mina records Omar as delayed if he has not paid.
9. Mina prepares payout.
10. Omar records money released outside ChopDot if assigned.
11. Leo confirms the release.
12. Mina closes the round.
13. Leo and Mina both review the receipt.

Pass if:

- members understand amount, treasurer, and payout recipient before acting;
- Mina can work the queue in order;
- the group sees Omar's delay before payout/closeout;
- Leo understands payout confirmation is separate from contribution claim;
- the receipt shows confirmed contributions, notes, release, and closeout state.

Fail if:

- the payout feels automatic or guaranteed;
- Omar's delay disappears from the final record;
- Leo believes his own `Mark paid` action confirmed receipt;
- anyone describes ChopDot as holding the circle funds.

## Scenario 3: Emergency Pot

Job: coordinate urgent support with dignity, privacy, approval, and a redacted receipt.

People:

- Casey contributes.
- Morgan contributes or remains pending.
- Riley organizes and confirms support.
- Taylor approves release.
- Jordan receives support.

Steps:

1. Casey opens the emergency pot.
2. Casey says what details are visible and what details are hidden.
3. Casey clicks `Mark paid`.
4. Riley opens from a separate device.
5. Riley explains the organizer queue and confirms Casey only if money arrived.
6. Taylor opens before the pot is ready.
7. Taylor explains why approval is not available yet.
8. Riley confirms or records Morgan.
9. Riley prepares release.
10. Riley approves if required.
11. Taylor approves release readiness.
12. Riley or assigned payer marks released outside ChopDot.
13. Jordan confirms receipt.
14. Riley closes the pot and opens the receipt.

Pass if:

- Casey can contribute without seeing sensitive emergency details;
- Taylor can say `approval comes after contributions are handled`;
- Jordan can say `I confirm after release is marked`;
- Riley understands confirmation is only for money actually received;
- the receipt is redacted and excludes sensitive names, reasons, private notes, and payment refs.

Fail if:

- anyone sees private medical details in the normal flow or receipt;
- Taylor thinks approval means payment happened;
- Jordan is asked to confirm before release is marked;
- the group believes ChopDot is custody, escrow, or guaranteed payout.

## Scenario 4: Community Fund

Job: manage a shared fund period with contributions, approvals, release, receiver confirmation, and treasurer handoff.

People:

- Sam contributes and may record the release.
- Noor contributes.
- Alex is admin/treasurer.
- Priya is approver.
- Jordan receives the release.

Steps:

1. Sam opens the community fund and marks contribution paid.
2. Priya opens before release approval is ready.
3. Priya explains why approval is not available yet.
4. Alex opens and reads the organizer queue.
5. Alex confirms Sam.
6. Noor contributes from a separate device.
7. Alex confirms Noor.
8. Alex prepares release.
9. Alex approves release readiness.
10. Priya approves release readiness.
11. Sam records money released outside ChopDot.
12. Jordan confirms receipt.
13. Alex closes the period and exports/reviews the receipt.

Pass if:

- Sam understands contribution first, release later;
- Priya understands approval is not payment;
- Alex can explain the queue;
- Jordan understands receiver confirmation;
- the receipt can be used as a handoff record for the next treasurer.

Fail if:

- Priya approves before contributions are handled;
- Sam can release money before approvals;
- Jordan's confirmation is skipped;
- the handoff record does not show unresolved items or evidence refs.

## Scenario 5: Capture / Pay / Confirm

Job: capture a pay moment quickly and close the loop without confusing payer claim with receiver confirmation.

People:

- Mina records a spend.
- Leo receives a pay link.
- Mina receives a confirm link or opens the pot.

Steps:

1. Mina starts from the app and creates a spend/split.
2. Mina shares or opens a `/pay?t=` link for Leo.
3. Leo opens the link and explains what `Mark paid` means.
4. Leo marks paid.
5. Mina opens `/confirm?t=` or the pot.
6. Mina confirms only if money arrived.
7. Both people review the pot status.

Pass if:

- Leo understands the link marks a payment claim only;
- Mina understands confirm means money arrived;
- the pot does not close from Leo's claim alone;
- a consumed confirm link does not strand the user on an error page.

Fail if:

- the pay link appears to move money inside ChopDot;
- confirmation is available to the wrong person;
- the group cannot find the resulting pot record.

## Scenario 6: Onboarding / First Entry

Job: prove that a new person can enter ChopDot without thinking a wallet is required for coordination.

Steps:

1. New participant opens the app cold.
2. Ask: "What can you do before connecting a wallet?"
3. Participant continues as guest.
4. Participant opens a pot.
5. Participant signs out and returns to onboarding.

Pass if:

- user understands coordination can start before wallet connection;
- wallet is understood as optional for settlement references/archive/proof;
- guest user reaches pots without wallet-required copy;
- sign-out returns to onboarding.

Fail if:

- user thinks a wallet is mandatory to coordinate;
- onboarding feels like a crypto app before a group-money app;
- sign-out creates confusion or a dead end.

## Scenario 7: Polkadot-Native Boundaries

Job: prove the app does not overclaim live `.dot` or host-native readiness while blocked externally.

Steps:

1. Show the native session status or receipt archive/proof boundary.
2. Ask: "What is live here, and what is blocked?"
3. Ask: "Does chain/token evidence confirm payment by itself?"
4. Ask: "Does this app hold funds?"

Pass if:

- user understands live `.dot`/host proof is blocked or gated;
- user understands token/chain evidence is supporting evidence only;
- user says confirmation still comes from the person receiving money;
- user does not infer custody or escrow;
- user can say `ChopDot is not holding funds`.

Fail if:

- user believes `.dot` live proof is complete today;
- user believes Asset Hub/token evidence confirms payment by itself;
- user believes ChopDot holds or protects the money.

## Promotion Rules

Group expenses can reach 9/10 when:

- three real users complete the flow;
- no one confuses claim with confirmation;
- receipt comprehension passes.

Savings circles can reach 9/10 when:

- treasurer and two members complete a round;
- delay handling is understood;
- payout is understood as external and not guaranteed.

Emergency pots can reach 9/10 when:

- contributor, organizer, approver, and recipient all pass;
- redaction is verified;
- no one infers custody, public emergency details, or automatic payout.

Community funds can reach 9/10 when:

- contributor, admin, approver, payer, and receiver all pass;
- approval is understood as readiness, not payment;
- handoff record is useful to a next treasurer.

Capture/pay/confirm can reach 9/10 when:

- payer and receiver both pass;
- pay link does not imply money moved inside ChopDot;
- confirm link cleanly returns to usable product state.

Auth/onboarding can reach 9/10 when:

- new users understand guest-first coordination;
- wallet/provider paths are visible but not required;
- configured provider proof exists for non-guest paths.

Polkadot-native adapter paths can reach 9/10 only when:

- host-required Product Account, Statement Store, Bulletin/archive, Asset Hub evidence, and closeout proof gates pass without local fallbacks;
- live `.dot`/host blockers are resolved;
- no host-dependent item is counted as done before that.
